\section{Motivation}
Early flood warning systems rely on timely information about water levels to alert users before critical thresholds are reached. Early warning systems can help mitigate the impact of floods by allowing for timely actions. With the high occurrence of floods across Europe in recent decades  \cite{bloschlCurrentEuropeanFloodrich2020}, there is a genuine need for accurate and timely alerts. FloodAlert \cite{FloodAlertWaterLevels} provides a service by offering users the possibility to subscribe to water level alerts for specific locations. Furthermore, FloodAlert offers a web interface for viewing real-time water level data from monitoring stations around the world (available at \url{https://pegelalarm.at/paw/}). The availability of real-time and historical water level data creates the opportunity to extend the service with a short-term water level forecasting feature, which could provide users with additional information about the expected water levels in the near future and potentially enable earlier alerts. This thesis aims to investigate the potential of short-term water level forecasting for FloodAlert.

Currently, FloodAlerts relies on a simple threshold-based alerting system, which triggers alerts when water levels exceed a predefined threshold. FloodAlert does not own or operate any measurement stations, it merely collects the data from various sources and provides a single interface for measurement stations all around the world.
