\section{Motivation}
Early flood warning systems rely on timely information about water levels to alert users before critical thresholds are reached. Early warning systems can help mitigate the impact of floods by allowing for timely actions. With the high occurrence of floods across Europe in recent decades  \cite{bloschlCurrentEuropeanFloodrich2020}, there is a genuine need for accurate and timely alerts. FloodAlert \cite{FloodAlertWaterLevels} provides a service by offering users the possibility to subscribe to water level alerts for specific locations. Furthermore, FloodAlert offers a web interface for viewing real-time water level data from monitoring stations around the world (available at \url{https://pegelalarm.at/paw/}). The availability of real-time and historical water level data creates the opportunity to extend the service with a short-term water level forecasting feature, which could provide users with additional information about the expected water levels in the near future and potentially enable earlier alerts. This thesis aims to investigate the potential of short-term water level forecasting for FloodAlert.

Currently, FloodAlert relies on a simple threshold-based alerting system, which triggers alerts when water levels exceed a predefined threshold. FloodAlert does not own or operate any measurement stations, it merely collects the data from various sources and provides a single interface for measurement stations all around the world.

\section{Aim and Scope}
The aim is to investigate the potential of short-term water level forecasting for FloodAlert. The initial goal is not to provide a fully operational flood warning system based on forecasted values, but rather to provide users with an indication of the possible future trend of water levels on the website. This distinction is important because the forecast should provide reliable predictions across different water level conditions. However, it is expected that model performance will decrease for extreme water levels, as such events are less frequent and therefore less represented in the available data. The scope of this thesis is to focus on a single station to reduce the complexity of the forecasting task and allow for a detailed evaluation of different approaches. The insights gained from this evaluation can serve as a basis for assessing the feasibility of extending the approach to multiple stations. The results can then be used to assess whether extending the forecasting approach to multiple stations is feasible. Although results from a single station cannot directly be generalized to other stations, they provide a first indication of the achievable forecasting performance, the required data engineering efforts, and the overall feasibility of scaling the approach.
