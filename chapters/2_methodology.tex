\section{Water Level Data}
The main data source for the thesis is the FloodAlert water level data \cite{FloodAlertWaterLevels}. To limit the scope of the thesis a single station is selected as the forecasting target. The target station is on the Danube and located in Korneuburg (a suburb of Vienna). The main reasons for selecting this station are the availability and quality of the data, as well as its popularity 
(measured by notification subscribers). % TODO Check with Johannes if I can include that
Additionally the station has multiple upstream measurement stations, which allows for the investigation of the effect of upstream data on forecast performance. 

Each station provides at most two features: water level and flow rate. Since not every station provides both features, the flow rate has been dismissed as a feature and just the water level is used. The resolution of the historical water level data is hourly, only with the last 90 days being available in ``raw'' (mostly 15-minute) resolution dating back to August 4, 2014 for the target station. 

\section{Weather Data}
The weather data was fetched from GeoSphere Austria using the \ac{INCA}-v1 dataset \cite{geosphereaustriaINCAv11Meteorologische2021}. The GeoSphere \ac{INCA}-v1 dataset \cite{geosphereaustriaINCAv11Meteorologische2021} was used because it provides hourly historical weather data dating back to March 15, 2011, with a spatial resolution of 1 km. Another reason for using this dataset was the hourly update frequency, thus making it suitable for real-time forecasting. The dataset contains several weather variables like temperature, precipitation, wind speed, and humidity. Precipitation and temperature were selected as the weather variables.

\section{Data Preprocessing}
\label{sec:data-preprocessing}
The preprocessing pipeline consists of four main stages: the water level data is cleaned, weather data is integrated with the water level observations, the individual station datasets are merged into a single data frame, and finally the dataset is split into training and test sets.

The water level data is fetched from the FloodAlert API \cite{SOBOSGmbHPublic_waterlevel_api_doc2026}. The measurements are aligned to an hourly temporal resolution, and timestamps are converted to UTC. Invalid measurements, such as values of -9999 or 0, are replaced with missing values, only positive water level values are considered valid. Subsequently, short missing-value gaps of up to six hours are filled using linear interpolation, while longer gaps remain missing. Additionally, an indicator column is added to record whether individual values are imputed.

The weather data \cite{geosphereaustriaINCAv11Meteorologische2021} is then retrieved based on the geographical location of each measurement station and joined with the processed water level data. A left join is used to preserve all water level observations, even if corresponding weather measurements are unavailable.

Afterwards, the individual station datasets containing water level and weather data are merged into a single data frame, provided that the station timeline overlaps with the target station by at least 90\%, to ensure sufficient temporal coverage of upstream station features. Two out of ten upstream stations do not meet this criterion and are therefore excluded.

The final preprocessing step is the chronological split into training and test datasets. The training dataset contains the first 80\% of the temporal range, while the remaining 20\% is reserved for testing. The split is based on timestamps rather than the number of valid water level observations, such that the training and test sets are defined by fixed time periods and not by the amount of available data. The split is performed in the preprocessing stage to ensure that the test dataset remains unseen thereby preventing data leakage during feature engineering and model training.
