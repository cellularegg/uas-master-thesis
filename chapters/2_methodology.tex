\section{End-to-end Pipeline}
\autoref{fig:methodological-pipeline} summarizes the pipeline used in this thesis. Water level and meteorological data are fetched from the respective sources and are preprocessed independently. The data are then chronologically split into training and test sets. Feature engineering is performed separately on both the training and test set to avoid data leakage. Forecasting models are trained and evaluated using expanding window \ac{CV} on the training set. Model selection is solely based on the \ac{CV} results, the test set is only used for the final evaluation of the selected model.

\section{Weather Data}
The weather data was fetched from GeoSphere Austria using the \ac{INCA}-v1 dataset \cite{geosphereaustriaINCAv11Meteorologische2021}. The GeoSphere \ac{INCA}-v1 dataset \cite{geosphereaustriaINCAv11Meteorologische2021} was used because it provides hourly historical weather data dating back to March 15, 2011, with a spatial resolution of 1 km. Another reason for using this dataset was the hourly update frequency, thus making it suitable for real-time forecasting. The dataset contains several weather variables like temperature, precipitation, wind speed, and humidity. Precipitation and temperature were selected as the weather variables.

\section{Data Preprocessing}
\label{sec:data-preprocessing}
The preprocessing pipeline consists of four main stages: the water level data is cleaned, weather data is integrated with the water level observations, the individual station datasets are merged into a single data frame, and finally the dataset is split into training and test sets.

The water level data is fetched from the FloodAlert API \cite{SOBOSGmbHPublic_waterlevel_api_doc2026}. The measurements are aligned to an hourly temporal resolution, and timestamps are converted to UTC. Invalid measurements, such as values of -9999 or 0, are replaced with missing values, only positive water level values are considered valid. Subsequently, short missing-value gaps of up to twelve hours are filled using forward fill, while longer gaps remain missing. Additionally, an indicator column is added to record whether individual values are imputed.

The weather data \cite{geosphereaustriaINCAv11Meteorologische2021} is then retrieved based on the geographical location of each measurement station and joined with the processed water level data. A left join is used to preserve all water level observations, even if corresponding weather measurements are unavailable.

Afterwards, the individual station datasets containing water level and weather data are merged into a single data frame, provided that the station timeline overlaps with the target station by at least 90\%, to ensure sufficient temporal coverage of upstream station features. Two out of nine upstream stations do not meet this criterion and are therefore excluded.

The final preprocessing step is the chronological split into training and test datasets. The training dataset contains the first 80\% of the temporal range, while the remaining 20\% is reserved for testing. The split is based on timestamps rather than the number of valid water level observations, such that the training and test sets are defined by fixed time periods and not by the amount of available data. The split is performed in the preprocessing stage to ensure that the test dataset remains unseen thereby preventing data leakage during feature engineering and model training.

\section{Feature Engineering}
\label{sec:feature-engineering}
The feature engineering step creates additional features from the preprocessed data. The engineered features are divided into the following categories:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Lag: Past water level values.
  \item Change features: Differences between current and past water levels.
  \item Rolling statistics: Mean, standard deviation, and other summary statistics over a rolling window.
  \item Weather summaries: Aggregated weather data over a rolling window, such as total precipitation or average temperature.
  \item Quality indicators: Number of imputed values in the past x hours
  \item Calendar features: Encoding of the timestamp into features such as hour of the day, day of the week.
\end{enumerate}
To not explode the feature space only the target station gets the additional features calculated. Other upstream stations only provide their current water level, imputation indicator, and weather variables as additional inputs. The final step of the feature engineering is to add a target column for each hour of the forecast horizon. This makes it possible to train and test the models based on the entire forecast horizon. If any of the target values are invalid, the flag is set to false and the sample is subsequently excluded from model training and evaluation.

\section{Forecasting Strategy}
The general approach is to use a \ac{MIMO} strategy. This means that a single model predicts multiple future time steps at once, rather than using a recursive strategy where the model predicts one time step at a time and feeds the prediction back into the model for the next time step. The \ac{MIMO} strategy is chosen to mitigate the error accumulation that can occur with recursive strategies \cite{bentaiebReviewComparisonStrategies2012}. \autoref{eq:mimo-forecasting} shows the concept of a \ac{MIMO} forecasting model, where $X_t$ is the input data at time $t$, $\hat{y}_{t+x}$ is the predicted water level at time $t+x$, and $H$ is the forecast horizon. The forecast horizon is set to 24 hours.
\begin{equation}
  X_t \longrightarrow \text{Model} \longrightarrow [\hat{y}_{t+1}, \hat{y}_{t+2}, \ldots, \hat{y}_{t+H}]
  \label{eq:mimo-forecasting}
\end{equation}
Another reason for choosing a \ac{MIMO} approach is to reduce the number of models, thus also reducing training time and complexity of the system. If the goal is to roll out the system to multiple stations, training and inference time should be kept as low as possible.

\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:experimental-design}
The dataset is split into training and test sets (80/20), the test set is only used for the final evaluation, while the training set is used for training, hyperparameter tuning, feature selection and model selection. Thereby ensuring that the test set remains completely unseen during the model development process.  Expanding window \ac{CV} is used to evaluate different hyperparameter and features \cite{hewamalageForecastEvaluationData2023}. \autoref{fig:expanding-window-cv} shows the exact \ac{CV} setup, 50\% of the training data is used for training in the first fold and about 10\% of the training data is used for validation in each fold. The training set grows by about 10\% in each fold. Between the folds' training and validation sets there is a gap of 24 hours (equal to the forecast horizon) to prevent data leakage. Before training the rows are checked for eligibility. This check contains the following conditions: all target values must exist and not be imputed and all features must be valid (not missing), this also includes features not used in the current feature subset. This decision has been made to provide a fair comparison between the different subsets regardless of the number of available rows per feature. % Todo make this sentence more clear

Only the RNN is not trained on all feature subsets. Instead, it is trained exclusively on the raw all-stations feature subset. This choice is made both to reduce the computational cost and training time and because the RNN is specifically designed to learn temporal dependencies directly from sequences of past observations. Except for ridge regression all models are tuned using randomized grid search. \cite{bergstraRandomSearchHyperParameter2012, feurerHyperparameterOptimization2019}

MlFlow is used to manage the experiments and track the results. After the \ac{CV} the best model is selected based on the mean \ac{RMSE} across all folds.

\section{Models}
The baseline model is a persistence model, which predicts the last observed water level for each future time step \cite{hyndmanAnotherLookMeasures2006, makridakisM4Competition1000002020}. Ridge regression is included as a linear model. Two tree-based approaches are considered: XGBoost and Extra Trees. Extra Trees is used instead of Random Forest because its additional randomization can reduce computational cost while providing a comparable tree-ensemble approach \cite{geurtsExtremelyRandomizedTrees2006}. Finally, two neural-network approaches are included: a feed-forward \ac{MLP} and an \ac{RNN} using either \ac{GRU} or \ac{LSTM} cells.

\section{Model Evaluation}
The main performance measure is the \ac{RMSE}; additionally, \ac{MAE}, \ac{ME}, and \R are reported. \ac{RMSE} is chosen as the primary metric because it penalizes larger prediction errors more strongly. The other metrics provide additional information about model performance. \ac{MAE} is less sensitive to large errors than \ac{RMSE}, \ac{ME} provides information about systematic prediction bias, and \R indicates how much of the variance in the observed water levels is explained by the model. Each metric is calculated separately for every forecast horizon step and additionally across the entire forecast horizon. Furthermore, the metrics are calculated separately for the Q1--Q4 ranges of the actual water levels to evaluate model performance under different water-level conditions. The quartile thresholds are calculated based on the training data only. As an additional diagnostic, the metrics are also calculated for observations at or above 545 cm, the default alarm threshold for the target station, to evaluate model performance during high water events.
